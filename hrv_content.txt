What is HRV? Heart Rate Variability (HRV) measures the small changes between each heartbeat. It reflects how well your autonomic nervous system (ANS) — which controls stress, heart rate, and recovery — is functioning. Higher HRV means your body adapts better to stress, while lower HRV can indicate imbalance, fatigue, or early signs of disease. Why HRV Matters HRV gives early warning signs of how stress affects your heart, brain, and metabolism. Doctors use HRV to: Detect nerve-related heart problems in people with diabetes. Track stress imbalance linked to hypertension, anxiety, or sleep issues. Monitor recovery after heart attack or rehabilitation. Evaluate how lifestyle changes or therapies improve overall resilience. Support preventive care and stress management programs. Who Can Get an HRV StressCheck?
